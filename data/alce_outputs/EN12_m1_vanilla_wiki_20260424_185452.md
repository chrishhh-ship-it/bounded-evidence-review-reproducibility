Based solely on the provided evidence, user trust in AI-generated literature reviews is determined by factors related to **algorithmic bias** and **algorithmic amplification**, which can introduce systematic and harmful tendencies or unfairly privilege certain content [5][4]. Specifically, bias can emerge from intentionally biased design decisions or from the unintended use of data used to train the algorithm [5]. Furthermore, algorithmic amplification can influence the distribution of information by increasing the visibility of certain content, which could skew a literature review [4].

**Structural auditability** can address these trust factors by enabling the monitoring of AI systems for risks and enhancing their robustness, which are core concerns of the AI safety field [1]. By making the system's design choices and data selection processes transparent, auditability allows for the identification and mitigation of algorithmic bias [5] and helps ensure that the AI system behaves as intended [1]. This aligns with the goal of AI alignment, which aims to ensure AI systems produce outcomes that are not harmful or systematically unfair [1]. In essence, structural auditability provides a mechanism to verify that the literature review is not being unduly influenced by biased data or amplification effects, thereby building user trust through verifiable safety and fairness.
