User trust in AI-generated literature reviews is fundamentally shaped by perceptions of reliability, fairness, and safety. Key factors include concerns about **algorithmic bias**, which can produce systematic and unfair outcomes by privileging one category over another [5], and **algorithmic amplification**, where automated ranking systems may disproportionately increase the visibility of certain content [4]. Users also worry about the potential for accidents, misuse, or harmful consequences, which is the core focus of **AI safety** research [1]. Trust is further eroded when the AI's decision-making processes are opaque, as users cannot verify the selection or weighting of sources.

**Structural auditability** addresses these factors by providing a transparent framework for oversight. By making the AI's internal processes—such as data selection, ranking algorithms, and bias mitigation steps—open to inspection, auditability allows users and regulators to verify that the system behaves as intended [1]. This directly counters algorithmic bias by enabling the detection of unfair outcomes [5] and helps monitor for harmful algorithmic amplification [4]. Furthermore, auditability supports the development of norms and policies that promote safety [1], as it creates accountability for the system's outputs. In essence, structural auditability transforms an opaque "black box" into a verifiable system, thereby rebuilding user trust through transparency and demonstrable alignment with intended behaviors.
