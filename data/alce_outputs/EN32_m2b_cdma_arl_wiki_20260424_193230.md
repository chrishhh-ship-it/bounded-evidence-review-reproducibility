Machine learning (ML) methods can detect reporting bias and publication bias in biomedical evidence synthesis primarily by leveraging high-quality training datasets [4] and advanced algorithms like deep learning [2]. In the context of systematic reviews and meta-analyses, which synthesize quantitative data from multiple studies to compute combined effect sizes [3], ML can be trained to identify patterns indicative of bias. For instance, deep learning, a subdiscipline of ML that uses multilayered neural networks [1][2], can be applied to analyze the distribution of published effect sizes and variance measures extracted from studies [3]. By learning from labeled datasets [4], these models can detect anomalies such as an overabundance of positive results or missing negative findings, which are hallmarks of publication bias. Furthermore, ML algorithms can automate the critical appraisal of study quality during systematic reviews [5], flagging studies with selective outcome reporting or methodological flaws that contribute to reporting bias. The ability of ML to generalize from training data [1] allows these detection systems to improve over time as more high-quality datasets become available [4]. Ultimately, integrating ML into the meta-analytic process [3] enhances the statistical power to resolve uncertainties [3] and provides a more objective, scalable approach to identifying biases that might otherwise compromise the validity of evidence-based conclusions [5].
