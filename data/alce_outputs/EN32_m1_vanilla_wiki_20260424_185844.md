Machine learning (ML) methods can detect reporting bias and publication bias in biomedical evidence synthesis by leveraging statistical algorithms that learn from data [1]. In the context of evidence synthesis, a systematic review extracts and interprets data from published studies to produce refined conclusions [5], while meta-analysis combines quantitative data from multiple studies to compute a combined effect size [3]. ML can be applied to these processes to identify discrepancies that signal bias.

Specifically, deep learning (DL), a subdiscipline of ML that uses multilayered neural networks [2], can be trained on high-quality labeled datasets [4] to classify or predict patterns indicative of bias. For example, ML algorithms can analyze the distribution of effect sizes and variance measures extracted from studies during meta-analysis [3]. If the combined effect size is skewed due to missing negative results (publication bias) or selective outcome reporting (reporting bias), ML models can detect anomalies by comparing observed data against expected distributions learned from training data [4]. Advances in DL have allowed neural networks to surpass previous approaches in performance [1], enabling more sensitive detection of subtle bias patterns in large-scale systematic reviews [5]. By automating the identification of these biases, ML enhances the reliability of evidence synthesis, ensuring that conclusions are based on a complete and unbiased set of studies.
