Climate adaptation policies address equity and justice concerns by focusing on the unequal impacts of climate change on marginalized or vulnerable populations. Climate justice, a type of environmental justice, seeks an equitable distribution of both the burdens of climate change and the efforts to mitigate it through advocacy and policy change [1]. Adaptation, defined as the process of adjusting to current and anticipated climate effects, aims to moderate or avoid harm for people [2]. This directly relates to vulnerable communities, as climate change vulnerability describes the "propensity or predisposition to be adversely affected" by climate change, with issues around the capacity to cope and adapt being central to the concept [5]. Economic analysis of climate change can guide adaptation policies by calculating the scale and distribution of damages, making explicit the trade-offs between impacts, adaptation, and mitigation [3]. For example, in a cost–benefit analysis, these trade-offs are clarified to ensure that adaptation efforts do not disproportionately burden vulnerable groups [3]. However, current research highlights that policies must actively address disparities, as the economic burden of climate change mitigation is estimated at around 1% to 2% of GDP [1].
