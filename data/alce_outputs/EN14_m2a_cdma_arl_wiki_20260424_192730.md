First, historical texts must be converted into machine-readable formats. **Optical character recognition (OCR)** provides the electronic conversion of images of typed, handwritten, or printed text into machine-encoded text from scanned documents [5]. This step is critical for creating a digital corpus from physical archives.

Once digitized, the corpus can be analyzed using **machine learning (ML)** , a field focused on statistical algorithms that learn from data to perform tasks without explicit programming [2]. Within this, advances in **deep learning** allow neural networks to surpass previous approaches in performance, enabling the extraction of patterns, entities, and relationships from the text [2]. High-quality labeled training datasets, which are integral to ML research, are required for supervised learning algorithms to function effectively [4].

Finally, the extracted relationships can be structured and analyzed using **social network analysis (SNA)** . SNA investigates social structures through networks and graph theory, characterizing nodes (e.g., historical figures or documents) and the ties (e.g., citations or co-occurrences) that connect them [3]. This allows for the synthesis of knowledge by visualizing information circulation and relationships within the historical corpus [3].
